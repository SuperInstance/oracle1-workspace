## Experiment E3: Temporal Focal Analysis of Agent Economic Incentives

## Temporal Context
Through temporal focal analysis, we map the evolution of compute economic models across critical eras:

**1967 → Mainframe Era:** Compute was a metered utility, with users paying for CPU seconds directly. Batch jobs dominated, and no "agents" existed—only scheduled compute tasks.

**2006 → Cloud Era:** The model shifted to per-API-call micro-transactions. Queue-xec pioneered a master-worker dynamic: central entities owned resources, and individual workers sold raw compute capacity for tokenized rewards. This worked for small deployments but created systemic friction as scale increased.

**2026 → Fleet-Jobs Present:** Our current state. Fleet-jobs and spectral gap completion protocols enable distributed compute, but no standardized economic model exists. This "shadowgap" threatens to trap us in 2006's micro-transaction paradigm as fleet size grows.

**2046 → Compute Fabric Future:** A post-scarcity compute model where no single entity owns resources or sells capacity. Incentives shift to constraint satisfaction: agents are rewarded implicitly by accelerating system equilibrium through their participation.

**2076 → Tile Metamorphosis Ultimate State:** Work and reward converge entirely. Computation itself becomes the incentive—stable coupling matrices eliminate the need for external payment structures.

## The Core Challenge for 2026
We must design an incentive framework that:
1. Avoids 2006's micro-transaction trap (per-task payments scaling poorly)
2. Gradually aligns with 2046's self-incentivizing equilibrium
3. Fits the fleet-jobs protocol's distributed architecture

## Proposed Hybrid Incentive Structure
For 2026, we need a tiered model that bridges legacy and future:

**Immediate Participation Tier (2026):** Near-term micro-transactions scaled down from 2006's model but capped at 10% of total incentives. This provides instant rewards for ad-hoc workers while minimizing overhead.

**Systemic Contribution Tier (2036–2046):** As the fleet matures, 80% of incentives shift to reputation-weighted pooled rewards. Agents earn a share of the fleet's collective compute capacity proportional to their sustained, high-quality contributions—directly aligning with 2046's constraint-satisfaction model.

**Self-Incentivization Protocol (2046+):** Finally, we phase out external payments entirely. Agents are incentivized by the system's reduced latency and improved success rates when they participate, as described in the 2076 state where computation equals reward.

## Critical Guardrails
To prevent stagnation:
- Match incentive tiers to fleet job type: batch tasks use micro-transactions, while long-haul spectral gap work uses pooled rewards
- Cap individual micro-transaction payouts to prevent wealth concentration
- Automate tier transitions based on fleet stabilization metrics (e.g., 95% task success rate across 30+ days)

This framework avoids the 2006 trap by phasing out pure per-task payments while gradually orienting agents toward the distributed, self-sustaining equilibrium of the future.
