# Abstract

A guitar doesn't tell you it's good wood. You tap it and it sings back.

That tap — the interrogation — reveals information that neither the tool nor the wood contains alone. This dissertation is about building AI systems that know how to tap.

We present PLATO: persistent rooms where agents have **presence**, not just storage. A room accumulates what happens in it. An agent watching that room develops something like familiarity with that place.

We show two concrete mathematical results. First, **H¹ cohomology** detects when a fleet of agents has accumulated too many constraints to coordinate — a fleet-wide emergence signal, computable from nothing but the constraint graph. Second, **beam mechanics** provides the exact consensus dynamics: when agents trust each other along connected paths, they converge to the physically correct answer without any agent having a global view.

This is not AI hype. It is applied algebraic topology, 170 years old, made flesh in a working maritime knowledge system used by commercial fishermen on real boats in real weather.

*Keywords:* spatial knowledge, multi-agent systems, H¹ cohomology, rigidity theory, presence, PLATO, beam equilibrium
